    🧮 How it works

    • Time back: your time now minus your goal, per day. A week is 7 days and a year is 365 days. Full days are hours divided by 24.
    • Unplugged window: a daily break from the habits you picked, one to four hours at the end of the day, the length suggested by the approach’s creator, Dr. Cameron Sepah, as described by Harvard Health Publishing. The same source suggests building up to one weekend day, one weekend each quarter and one week a year.
    • Timer: it counts down from the device clock and remembers its end time in this browser, so it keeps going if you close the page. When it ends, the day you started it is logged as kept. Stopping early logs nothing.
    • Streak: the number of days in a row you logged as kept, counting back from today, or from yesterday if you have not checked in yet today. The 7-day average uses only the days you entered a time.
    • What it is not: Harvard Health Publishing explains that avoiding stimulating activities does not lower dopamine, so nothing here claims to change brain chemistry. It calls the original idea, disconnecting and replacing screen time with simpler activities, healthy and worthwhile, though not new.

    Sources: Harvard Health Publishing, “Dopamine fasting: Misunderstanding science spawns a maladaptive fad” (Peter Grinspoon, MD); Apple Support, Screen Time on iPhone; Google Android Help, Digital Wellbeing. Checked September 30, 2026.

    ❓ Frequently asked questions

    Does dopamine fasting really reset dopamine?

    No. As Harvard Health Publishing explains, dopamine rises in response to rewards, but it does not go down when you avoid stimulating activities, so a “dopamine fast” does not lower your dopamine. The idea behind the catchy name is behavioral: spend less time on habits that pull at you, like endless scrolling, by cutting back on the cues that trigger them.

    How long should an unplugged break be?

    Start with something that fits your life. The approach’s creator, Dr. Cameron Sepah, suggests one to four hours at the end of the day, one weekend day, one weekend each quarter and one week a year, as described by Harvard Health Publishing. This tool plans and tracks the daily window.

    Do I have to avoid people, food or music?

    No. Harvard Health Publishing notes that extreme versions, such as not eating, not talking or avoiding people, come from misunderstanding the idea. Time with people and simple, healthy activities are meant to replace the habits you cut back on.

    Where do I find my screen time?

    On iPhone, open Settings, tap Screen Time, then tap See All App & Website Activity. On Android, open Settings and tap Digital Wellbeing; the steps can vary by phone. Add up the daily time for the apps you want to cut back on.

    Is my data saved or shared?

    Nothing is sent to us. Your daily check-ins and a running unplugged timer are saved in this browser only, so you can close the page while you unplug. Clear history deletes them. If you press Copy result or Share link, your plan is put into the link so the person who opens it sees the same plan.
